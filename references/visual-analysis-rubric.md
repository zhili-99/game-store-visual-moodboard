# Visual analysis rubric

Use this rubric for full art-direction analysis. Prioritize repeated evidence across the set over one exceptional frame.

## 1. Sample structure

- Count files, unique hashes, orientations, and asset types.
- Identify campaign repeats, localization variants, gameplay screenshots, key art, and feature graphics.
- State what is in scope and what was excluded.

## 2. Emotional positioning

- Name the immediate emotion: comfort, excitement, mastery, urgency, humor, competition, danger, or aspiration.
- Identify the fantasy being sold: wealth, growth, rescue, collection, makeover, destruction, social status, or exploration.
- Distinguish emotional spectacle from mechanics communication.

## 3. Palette

- Separate persistent brand anchors from scene-dependent colors.
- Record HEX values for 6–10 useful tokens, not every extracted cluster.
- Explain each token's role: background, action, reward, danger, UI, neutral, or shadow.
- Check temperature, saturation, value range, complementary pairs, and exceptional dark-mode frames.

## 4. Composition and hierarchy

- Measure stable regions such as HUD, main event, footer, logo, CTA, or caption.
- Identify the focal object, direction of motion, camera angle, depth, and scale exaggeration.
- Note attention devices: hand cursors, arrows, glow, emoji, faces, explosions, coins, or warning shapes.

## 5. Rendering language

- Geometry: realistic, stylized, low-poly, toy-like, rounded, faceted, or graphic.
- Materials: matte, glossy plastic, metallic, translucent, soft skin, painterly, or textured.
- Lighting: diffuse, rim-lit, bloom-heavy, cinematic, flat, high-key, or low-key.
- Post-processing: saturation, contrast, vignette, depth of field, motion blur, and outline treatment.

## 6. Typography and UI

- Case, weight, width, slant, outline, shadow, line count, and placement.
- Copy pattern: action verb, benefit, problem, reward, threat, or social proof.
- Explain how HUD or footer elements unify otherwise different images.

## 7. Reusable art direction

Translate findings into rules another artist can execute:

- one frame = one promise;
- stable brand anchors;
- recommended color allocation;
- focal-scale and copy-length limits;
- rules for gameplay versus key-art lanes;
- explicit consistency risks and what must be harmonized.

Avoid vague labels such as “cartoon” or “colorful” unless followed by concrete visual evidence.
